# Figures 3–4

**Figure 3. National patterns of reported inhaler availability** **a**, Reported inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) availability in 2021. **b**, Counts of Yes, No and unknown answers for three medicine items, each retaining all 194 WHO country records. **c**, Regional matrices retain every country, with one column per country and one row per medicine. Country columns follow ICS, bronchodilator and combination answers, then ISO code. Blue, burgundy and grey indicate Yes, No and unknown, respectively. Combination denotes budesonide–formoterol. Answers concern general availability in public-sector primary care; No indicates failure to meet the survey threshold. These national reports do not measure individual treatment. Complete country-level source data accompany the figure.

**Figure 4. Joint medicine answers and historical ICS sequences** **a**, Three nested rings partition 157 countries with complete 2021 answers, from bronchodilator to ICS to budesonide–formoterol; angular widths represent country counts. **b**, All eight combinations, including zero-count patterns, with counts and percentages of 157. **c**, Historical ICS sequences in 2017, 2019 and 2021 crossed with combination answers in the 168-country panel; 144 have known combination answers. Cell shading represents counts. Y and N denote Yes and No. **d**, All three observed ICS-No/combination-Yes profiles: Algeria, Saint Kitts and Nevis, and Uzbekistan. Separate survey items cannot establish individual treatment regimens. Source data accompany the figure.
