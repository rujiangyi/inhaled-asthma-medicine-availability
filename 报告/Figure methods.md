# Figure methods

## Figures 5–6

The repeated panel contains 168 countries with Yes/No answers for ICS and the basic bronchodilator in 2017, 2019 and 2021. NNN and YYY indicate consistent No and Yes responses to the ICS question; the other six sequences are combined as Changed in Figure 6 only. Burden and population refer to residents aged 25 years or older in 2021. All calculations use the frozen country analysis table and statistical outputs; no model is re-estimated by the plotting script.

Figure 5(a) orders all 168 countries by descending adult asthma DALYs, breaking any ties by ISO3. At rank k, each coloured layer equals the cumulative DALYs from countries of that trajectory among the first k countries, divided by the DALYs in all 168 countries. The layer thicknesses sum to the cumulative total; at rank 168 they sum to 100%. The black outline shows that total. Top 10, top 25 and top 50 countries account for 64.1527%, 80.8911% and 91.5188%, respectively. Ranks refer to GBD point estimates, not rankings of treatment need or treatment effects. The plot contains no interpolated temporal values and no uncertainty intervals.

Figure 5(b) uses separate denominators within the same panel: 168 countries, 3,308,555,183.43 residents aged 25+, and 13,699,803.07 adult asthma DALYs. Country counts appear beside trajectory labels. Each trajectory's country count, population and DALY total is checked against the statistical output before rendering. Figure 5(c) divides each country's DALYs by the DALYs summed across the 35 NNN countries, with countries ordered by this contribution. Points show the original percentages on a logarithmic vertical axis, with a positive lower limit selected from the smallest observed contribution. Stems extend to that lower display limit; they do not represent intervals or a zero baseline. India contributes 68.7530% of NNN-group DALYs and Pakistan contributes 9.3%; both are annotated. The logarithmic axis makes smaller contributions visible without transforming their numerical labels. These are burdens among residents of classified countries, not estimates of untreated or treatment-attributable burden.

Figure 6 uses 2021 contextual data. PM2.5 is the population-weighted national annual mean ambient concentration in micrograms per cubic metre. GDP per capita is adjusted for purchasing-power parity (PPP), in constant 2021 international dollars (World Bank NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD). Current health expenditure per capita is in current PPP international dollars (SH.XPD.CHEX.PP.CD). Out-of-pocket expenditure is a percentage of current health expenditure. The GDP scatter axis and health-expenditure distribution axis are logarithmic. The contextual variables describe countries, whereas bubble populations refer specifically to ages 25+.

Figure 6(a) includes 160 countries with nonmissing PM2.5, GDP and population. Bubble area in squared typographic points equals 10 + 4 × square root(population aged 25+ in millions). This deliberate compression means area is not directly proportional to population; the size legend supplies reference populations. Countries with larger populations are drawn first to reduce occlusion of smaller observations.

Figure 6(b) retains all available observations for each plotted variable, rather than restricting every distribution to the 160-country matrix sample. Each of PM2.5, out-of-pocket share and health expenditure has 166 observed values: NNN 35, Changed 34 and YYY 97. Boxes show medians and interquartile ranges; whiskers extend to the most extreme values within 1.5 interquartile ranges. All observations, including those beyond the whiskers, are displayed as jittered points. Jitter has a fixed seed. The source data retain all 168 panel countries, missing values, inclusion flags and the names of missing contextual variables.

Figure 6(c) requires complete values for PM2.5, GDP, out-of-pocket share and health expenditure, yielding the same 160 countries as panel a. Within these countries, each indicator is ranked separately using the average rank for ties; the plotted percentile is 100 × rank / 160. No direction is reversed and no indicator weights, composite score, clustering, embedding or inferred links are used. Countries are ordered by ascending GDP, with ISO3 as the tie breaker; the four indicator rows and separate ICS strip use precisely this order. The GDP colour gradient is therefore a consequence of sorting. Colour denotes within-indicator percentile across countries; it does not compare original units across indicators or define a risk score. Omitted countries are identifiable in the full context source table and are not assigned imputed values.

Figures 5 and 6 use Times New Roman, including mathematical labels, black text and the shared trajectory/status palette. Outputs are vector PDF and SVG, 320-dpi PNG, 120-dpi preview, and panel-level source CSV files. Source CSVs for the current Figure 5 are trajectory shares, country cumulative contributions and NNN country contributions. Current Figure 6 sources are countries and context, scatter source, distribution summaries and aligned country percentiles.
